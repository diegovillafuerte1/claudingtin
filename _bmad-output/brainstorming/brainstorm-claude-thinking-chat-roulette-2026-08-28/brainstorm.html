Technique 02 · Invented

Two Clocks Ticking

A technique made up for this session, tuned to the concept's own physics.

the rule Every encounter runs on two invisible countdowns — your Claude, their Claude. Neither is shown. Either can fire at any moment, even mid-sentence. For each idea, ask: what does it do the instant a clock fires? Design mechanics, etiquette, UI and emotion that either exploit or soften the two-random-clocks structure.

their claude

The structure, made literal: whichever clock hits zero first ends the chat. No "they left." The spinner just returns and you silently re-enter the queue.

idea · default
Persist OFF: when your Claude finishes, the chat just ends and you forget about them. Clean. No decision to make.
idea
Persist ON: the chat keeps going past think-time. You stay until you choose to stop — anytime.
idea
"Persist" is a user setting that can be defaulted ON.
idea · emotional design
Plausible-deniability exit: leaving anytime is framed as "my Claude came back," so bailing never reads as rejection.
idea
On disconnect: just show the "searching" spinner again and silently re-enter the queue. No "they left" message.
direction
Resist feature bloat / minigames. "Does your downtime distractor need another minigame?" Keep it minimal.
idea
Fill dead beats with lightweight tooltips / nudges — e.g. "why not edit your profile."
idea
Open each match with an auto-generated icebreaker built from both profiles.
insight
Per-match generated icebreakers may be too slow / costly to compute at match time.
idea · fallback
Instead: an instant rotation of generic, pre-written opener prompts.
Technique 03 · Assumption Reversal

Flip a load-bearing assumption

Name something the design quietly takes for granted, then invert it.

assumed → reversed "Only waiting users can be matched." → What if an active, non-waiting user could be pulled into a match when they fit the searcher's target profile?
idea
Pull-in: active users can be matched if they fit the searcher's opt-in target criteria.
idea
Profile fields like a/s/l (age / sex / location) as match keys.
insight
a/s/l + targeted search makes it explicitly a pseudo dating app — the concept's recurring gravitational pull.
idea
Optional interest hashtags that lightly bias matching toward people sharing a tag — so you re-meet a small cast occasionally.
insight
Recurring-match risk: running into a creep tied to an interest would make you feel forced to drop the interest to escape them. You need a per-person block that doesn't cost you the tag.

Synthesis

Where the threads tie

The non-obvious connections — small pieces from three different techniques that turn out to hold each other up.

The social fiction is load-bearing

"My Claude came back" isn't flavor text — it's the mechanism that lets every exit and every disconnect happen without anyone reading it as rejection. Pair it with "no ‘they left’ message" and the whole ephemeral model becomes socially cheap. Remove the fiction and it collapses.

A three-rung commitment ramp

The persist toggle, the shareable session ID, and the missed-connections board aren't separate features — they're one escalating staircase, each rung a larger opt-in step away from "forget them" and toward "actually connect."

Pull-in is the cold-start fix

A FIFO queue only feels alive when it's full. Matching active, non-waiting users into encounters is what keeps the plugin working on day one and in the thin hours — the reversed assumption doubles as the bootstrap.

One block primitive kills three threats

A per-person block that doesn't cost you your tag answers all of it at once: the creep tied to an interest, the HelloTalk-style dating drift, and the small-pool recurring-match problem. Three worries, one control.

The cooldown is that same primitive, automated

"~1 minute together, then auto-block for a day" is just the manual block run on a timer — the safety control repurposed as a variety engine for pull-in and small pools.

Chosen direction

What we're actually building

the shape of it

  • A for-fun, open-source, Claude-integrated plugin — not a startup.
  • FIFO baseline pairing, plus optional profile-matched pull-in of active users.
  • A light dating-match element is acceptable — stop fighting the concept's pull.
  • Ephemeral-by-default chat with a persist toggle (can default ON).
  • Exits and disconnects framed as "my Claude came back"; on drop, just re-queue silently.
  • v2: companion webapp with session IDs + a missed-connections board.
Open design question for downstream Anti-repeat / cooldown rules for pull-in and small pools — e.g. rate-limit re-matches with the same person: ~1 minute together, then a temporary auto-block for a day to force variety. Exact rules still to be designed.
